## 0. Glossary of Key Terms

### Price & Performance Terms
| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **ATH (All-Time High)** | The highest price a stock has ever traded at. When a stock is "at ATH," it means it's at its most expensive point in history — there is no overhead resistance from prior bag-holders. |
| **52-Week High / Low** | The highest and lowest prices a stock has traded at over the past 12 months. Useful for gauging where the current price sits relative to its recent range. |
| **Market Cap (Market Capitalization)** | Total value of a company's outstanding shares = share price × total shares. E.g., a $200 stock with 1B shares = $200B market cap. Categorized as mega-cap ($200B+), large-cap ($10-200B), mid-cap ($2-10B), small-cap (<$2B). |
| **YTD (Year-to-Date)** | Performance from January 1 of the current year to today. "Up 117% YTD" means the stock has more than doubled since the start of 2026. |
| **YoY (Year-over-Year)** | Comparison to the same period last year. "Revenue +38% YoY" means revenue grew 38% compared to the same quarter one year ago. |
| **Intraday** | Within a single trading day. "Intraday high of $295" means the stock touched $295 at some point during today's session. |

### Technical Analysis Terms
| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **RSI (Relative Strength Index)** | A momentum oscillator ranging from 0-100 that measures the speed and magnitude of recent price changes. **Below 30 = oversold** (potentially undervalued, may bounce). **Above 70 = overbought** (potentially overvalued, may pull back). **40-60 = neutral.** RSI does NOT predict direction — a stock can stay overbought for weeks in a strong uptrend. |
| **MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)** | A trend-following indicator that shows the relationship between two moving averages of a stock's price (typically 12-day and 26-day). When the MACD line crosses above the signal line = **bullish (Buy signal)**. When it crosses below = **bearish (Sell signal)**. A positive MACD value (e.g., +4.08) suggests upward momentum. |
| **DMA (Day Moving Average)** | The average closing price over a set number of days, plotted as a line on the chart. It smooths out daily noise to show the underlying trend. |
| **50-DMA (50-Day Moving Average)** | The average closing price over the last 50 trading days (~2.5 months). Represents the **short-to-medium-term trend**. When the stock price is above the 50-DMA, the short-term trend is bullish. When below, it's bearish. |
| **200-DMA (200-Day Moving Average)** | The average closing price over the last 200 trading days (~10 months). Represents the **long-term trend**. Widely watched by institutional investors. A stock trading below its 200-DMA is considered to be in a long-term downtrend. |
| **Golden Cross** | When the 50-DMA crosses above the 200-DMA — a strongly bullish long-term signal. |
| **Death Cross** | When the 50-DMA crosses below the 200-DMA — a bearish long-term signal. |
| **Support Level** | A price floor where buying interest is strong enough to prevent the stock from falling further. Think of it as a "bounce zone." If a stock repeatedly bounces off $194, that's a support level. A break below support is bearish. |
| **Resistance Level** | A price ceiling where selling pressure prevents the stock from rising further. If a stock keeps failing to break above $430, that's resistance. A break above resistance is bullish and often triggers further buying. |
| **Consolidation** | When a stock trades sideways within a narrow range, neither trending up nor down. Often occurs after a big move as the market digests gains/losses before the next directional move. |
| **Pullback** | A temporary decline in price during an overall uptrend. Healthy pullbacks are normal — they allow overbought conditions to reset before the next leg up. |
| **IV Crush** | A sharp drop in options implied volatility after a known event (like earnings). Options lose value rapidly even if the stock moves in the right direction. |

### Valuation Terms
| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings)** | Stock price divided by earnings per share. Measures how much you pay for each dollar of profit. A P/E of 30x means you're paying $30 for every $1 of annual earnings. **Lower = cheaper, higher = more expensive.** Compare within the same sector — tech stocks typically have higher P/Es than banks. |
| **P/S Ratio (Price-to-Sales)** | Market cap divided by annual revenue. Useful for companies with low or no profits (like high-growth tech). A P/S of 10x means the market values the company at 10x its annual revenue. |
| **PEG Ratio (Price/Earnings-to-Growth)** | P/E ratio divided by the earnings growth rate. Adjusts the P/E for growth speed. **PEG < 1 = undervalued** relative to growth. **PEG > 2 = expensive** even accounting for growth. More useful than P/E alone for comparing fast-growing vs. slow-growing companies. |
| **EPS (Earnings Per Share)** | Net income divided by total shares outstanding. The "E" in P/E. When a company "beats EPS estimates," it means they earned more profit per share than analysts expected. |
| **Forward P/E** | P/E ratio using next year's estimated earnings (instead of trailing earnings). Shows what you're paying for future profits. Often lower than trailing P/E for growing companies. |
| **FCF (Free Cash Flow)** | Cash generated by the business after all operating expenses and capital expenditures. The actual cash a company produces — more reliable than accounting earnings because it's harder to manipulate. |

### Options & Trading Terms
| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Implied Volatility (IV)** | The market's forecast of how much a stock's price will move, derived from options prices. **High IV = market expects big move** (e.g., before earnings). Expressed as an annualized percentage. IV of 50% means the market expects the stock to move ~50%/year. |
| **Put/Call Ratio** | Number of put options traded divided by call options traded. **Below 0.7 = bullish** (more calls/bets on upside). **Above 1.0 = bearish** (more puts/bets on downside). Contrarians sometimes use extreme readings as reversal signals. |
| **Call Option** | A contract giving the right to BUY a stock at a set price (strike) by a set date. Traders buy calls when they're bullish. |
| **Put Option** | A contract giving the right to SELL a stock at a set price (strike) by a set date. Traders buy puts when they're bearish or want to hedge. |
| **Strike Price** | The price at which an option holder can buy (call) or sell (put) the underlying stock. "Heavy call buying at $240 strike" means traders are betting the stock will exceed $240. |
| **Open Interest** | Total number of outstanding option contracts that haven't been settled. High open interest at a strike price = significant market positioning at that level. |

### Institutional & Regulatory Terms
| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **13F Filing** | A quarterly SEC filing required from institutional investors managing $100M+. Reveals what stocks hedge funds, mutual funds, and pension funds own. Filed within 45 days of quarter-end, so data is always delayed. |
| **Form 4 (SEC)** | Filed when company insiders (executives, directors, 10%+ shareholders) buy or sell their own company's stock. Must be filed within 2 business days of the trade. Insider selling isn't always bearish (executives regularly sell for diversification), but clustered selling or unusual buying can be signals. |
| **Insider Trading (Legal)** | When company executives buy or sell their own stock following SEC disclosure rules. Distinct from illegal insider trading (trading on non-public information). |
| **FOMC (Federal Open Market Committee)** | The Fed committee that sets interest rate policy. Their decisions directly affect stock valuations — higher rates make future earnings worth less (hurting growth stocks), lower rates make them worth more. |

### Market Structure Terms
| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Sector Rotation** | When institutional money flows from one market sector to another. E.g., money moving from tech into energy. Often driven by economic cycle changes, rate expectations, or valuation shifts. |
| **Capex (Capital Expenditure)** | Money spent by companies on long-term assets like data centers, factories, or equipment. Hyperscaler capex = how much Amazon/Google/Microsoft/Meta spend building AI infrastructure. High capex benefits their suppliers (NVDA, AVGO, GLW). |
| **Mag 7 / Magnificent Seven** | The seven largest tech stocks by market cap: AAPL, MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, NVDA, META, TSLA. They dominate the S&P 500 index and heavily influence overall market performance. |
| **Concentration Risk** | The danger of having too much portfolio weight in a few correlated stocks. If the Mag 7 represent 34% of the S&P 500 and they all drop together, the entire index suffers disproportionately. |
| **Correlation** | How closely two stocks move together. Correlation of 1.0 = perfect lockstep. 0 = no relationship. -1.0 = opposite movement. A portfolio of highly correlated stocks provides less diversification. |
| **Equal-Weight vs. Cap-Weight** | Cap-weighted index (like S&P 500) gives bigger companies more influence. Equal-weight treats every stock the same. When cap-weight outperforms equal-weight by 32% over 3 years, it means the biggest stocks are carrying the market while the average stock lags. |
| **Consensus Target** | The average of all analyst price targets for a stock. "Consensus target $273" means the average Wall Street analyst expects the stock to reach $273 in the next 12 months. |
| **Buy / Hold / Sell Rating** | Analyst recommendations. **Buy** = expected to outperform the market. **Hold** = expected to perform in-line. **Sell** = expected to underperform. "Strong Buy" or "Outperform" are more emphatic versions of Buy. |

### Company-Specific Terms
| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Hyperscaler** | A company that operates massive cloud computing infrastructure at scale — primarily Amazon (AWS), Microsoft (Azure), Google (GCP), and Meta. Their spending decisions drive demand for chips, networking, and fiber optics. |
| **Foundry** | A semiconductor fabrication facility that manufactures chips designed by other companies. TSMC is the world's largest foundry — they make chips for Apple, Nvidia, AMD, and others. Intel is trying to become a foundry as well. |
| **IPO (Initial Public Offering)** | When a private company first sells shares to the public on a stock exchange. Anthropic, OpenAI, and Databricks are all expected to IPO in 2026. |
| **Valuation (Private)** | For private companies, the implied total value based on the price investors pay per share in funding rounds. Anthropic's "$900B valuation" means investors are buying shares at a price that values the whole company at $900B. |
